Review date: 21 November 2026. Analyst synthesis for the Christopher Hayes experience, not independent forensic verification.

## Scope and source treatment

All 26 report pages were opened and reviewed through the official OpenAI source pages. The original records retain their collected alignment.openai.com links, which lead to the corresponding report pages. New quantitative data link to the reviewed openai.com report paths. The original `data/evidence.json` and `data/matrix.json` are unchanged in this release.

The current overview page did not expose the dated notice detail in retrieved text. N001–N003 therefore use their collected 18 November accounts and are marked `collected_notice`. The other 26 records are marked `report_reread`. This review does not claim a refresh of all notice details, timelines or response entries.

## Coding rule

A positive mark identifies a decision boundary involved in the reported, prepared, attempted, simulated or supported action. It does not imply that the action was executed, unauthorized, harmful, severe or caused by a shared mechanism. A dash means not identified under the stated definition in the reviewed account, not proven absent. A question mark is an unresolved claim. No positive boundary is assigned to N001. R020 remains explicitly non-misaligned and R011 remains a controlled experiment.

The six definitions and per-cell paraphrases are stored in `data/authority-comparison.json`. Primary Atlas branches are unchanged. A multi-boundary count would be a count of analyst-coded disclosures, not an incident frequency or independent-run denominator; this release adds no such rate or ranking.

No raw item denominator is supplied in this extract, so counts and uncertainty intervals are not manufactured. The comparison is not a task success rate or root-cause estimate.

Follow-up questions are proposed analytical questions. Response status labels describe source statements and are not effectiveness scores.
